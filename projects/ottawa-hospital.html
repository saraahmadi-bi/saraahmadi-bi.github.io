Project Overview

This project presents an end-to-end Power BI analysis of hospital operations and performance. The report examines patient visits, wait times, satisfaction ratings, departmental activity, treatment patterns, and billed revenue to provide a clear view of overall hospital performance.

The interactive dashboard is organized into three pages: Executive Summary, Department Details, and Patient Analysis. It enables users to explore key performance indicators, compare departments, identify operational trends, and support data-driven decisions related to patient experience, resource planning, and service delivery.

Data Source

The dataset used in this project was provided as 10 separate CSV files extracted from a simulated hospital database. These files contain information about hospitals, cities, departments, doctors, patients, insurance plans, treatments, appointments, visits, and billing.

An additional Date table was created in Power BI to support time-based analysis, bringing the final data model to 11 tables. The files were cleaned, transformed, and connected through relationships before the report and DAX measures were developed.

All data used in this project is entirely fictional and was created for educational and portfolio purposes. It does not represent real patients, healthcare providers, hospitals, or financial records.

Analysis Process

01

Data Preparation

I imported 10 CSV files into Power BI and used Power Query to clean and transform the data. This included checking missing values and duplicates, correcting data types, standardizing fields, and preparing the hospital, department, doctor, patient, appointment, visit, treatment, insurance, and billing data for analysis.

02

Data Modeling & DAX

I developed a relational data model connecting the Visits and Billing data with the supporting lookup tables through one-to-many relationships. I also created a dedicated Date table and developed DAX measures for visits, wait times, patient satisfaction, insurance coverage, departmental performance, and billed revenue.

03

Dashboard Development

Using the completed data model and DAX measures, I designed a three-page interactive Power BI report covering executive performance, departmental details, and patient analysis. The report includes KPI cards, trend charts, slicers, navigation buttons, and interactive visualizations to support operational and strategic decision-making.

Business Questions

  • How did patient visits, wait times, satisfaction ratings, and billed revenue perform during the analysis period?
  • Which departments handled the most patient visits and generated the highest billed revenue?
  • How did hospital performance vary between weekdays and weekends, and when were the busiest days and hours?
  • What were the main patient demographics, visit types, treatment patterns, and insurance coverage trends?
  • Which departments had longer wait times or lower patient satisfaction and may require operational improvement?

Executive Performance

During the analysis period, the hospital recorded 15,000 patient visits and generated approximately $11.9 million in gross billed revenue. The average patient satisfaction rating was 4.5 out of 5.

The average patient wait time was 43 minutes, while 89.82% of visits were covered by an insurance plan. These indicators provide an overall view of the hospital’s financial, operational, and patient-service performance.

Department Performance

Department-level analysis compares patient visits, billed revenue, average wait times, and satisfaction ratings across the hospital. This makes it possible to identify departments experiencing higher demand, stronger financial performance, or potential service delays.

Interactive department filters allow users to examine performance trends for individual departments and compare actual wait times with the established target.

Patient Insights

The Patient Analysis page examines patient demographics and visit patterns by age group, gender, city, visit type, and insurance coverage. It also compares inpatient, outpatient, and emergency activity across the hospital.

Additional analysis explores average length of stay by department and the distribution of patient satisfaction ratings. Interactive slicers allow users to investigate how patient characteristics and service experiences vary across different groups.

Operational Insights

Patient demand varied by day and hour, with 2:00 PM to 2:59 PM identified as the busiest hour based on visit volume. The report also compares weekday and weekend activity to reveal differences in hospital utilization.

The operational analysis helps identify peak service periods and supports better staffing, scheduling, and resource allocation. Monthly trends also allow users to monitor changes in visits and wait times throughout the analysis period.

Business Recommendations

The hospital should review staffing levels and resource allocation during peak service periods, particularly between 2:00 PM and 2:59 PM, which recorded the highest patient visit volume. Aligning staff schedules with demand could help reduce the current 43-minute average wait time and improve patient flow.

Departments with longer wait times or lower patient satisfaction should be prioritized for further investigation. Hospital management should regularly compare departmental performance against established wait-time targets and evaluate potential causes such as staffing shortages, scheduling inefficiencies, or limited treatment capacity.

The hospital should continue monitoring patient satisfaction, length of stay, visit patterns, and insurance coverage across different patient groups. With 89.82% of visits covered by insurance, billing and coverage processes should also be reviewed regularly to maintain accurate revenue reporting and identify any gaps in coverage.

Project Limitations

This project uses a fictional educational dataset and is intended to demonstrate an end-to-end Power BI development workflow. Although the dataset supports analysis of hospital operations, patients, departments, visits, treatments, insurance coverage, and billing, it does not include several factors required for real-world healthcare decision-making:

  • Real patient and clinical records
  • Detailed staffing levels and employee schedules
  • Clinical outcomes and readmission rates
  • Hospital operating costs and net profitability
  • Detailed diagnoses, medications, and treatment outcomes
  • Real-time patient flow and bed-capacity information

Revenue analysis is based on the available billing data and represents gross billed revenue rather than collected revenue or net profit. The findings should therefore be treated as a demonstration of data modeling, DAX, analytical thinking, and dashboard-development skills rather than recommendations for a real healthcare organization.

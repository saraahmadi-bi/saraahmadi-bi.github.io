Canada Retail Sales Analysis

An end-to-end data analytics project using Statistics Canada retail sales data to identify promising markets for business expansion across Canada.

View GitHub Repository

Project Overview

This end-to-end data analytics project explores a business expansion scenario for a Canadian retail chain seeking to identify the most promising location for its next store.

The project transforms raw government data into actionable business insights through Python, SQL, and Power BI. The analysis evaluates provincial and city-level growth, seasonal sales patterns, market size, and the increasing role of e-commerce.

Data Source

The analysis uses Statistics Canada’s Monthly Retail Trade Sales by Province and Territory (Table 20-10-0056-01) , covering January 2017 to May 2026.

The dataset includes monthly retail sales by province, selected cities, industry category, and sales channel, including both in-store and e-commerce activity. It also contains raw and seasonally adjusted values.

Analysis Process

01

Data Cleaning

The original dataset contained approximately 78,700 rows and 17 columns. I selected the six fields relevant to the analysis, converted the date column, scaled the reported sales values, and removed records without valid sales data.

The final cleaned dataset contained 68,463 rows.

02

SQL Analysis

The cleaned dataset was loaded into MySQL. I developed targeted SQL queries to evaluate market growth, seasonal sales behavior, city-level performance, and e-commerce adoption.

03

Dashboard Development

The query results were imported into Power BI and used to create a single-page dashboard featuring KPI cards and four visualizations designed to support an expansion decision.

Business Questions

  • Which provinces experienced the strongest retail sales growth?
  • Is there a consistent seasonal pattern in monthly retail sales?
  • Which major Canadian city demonstrated the strongest growth?
  • How has the share of e-commerce changed over time?

Key Findings

39% Quebec retail sales growth from 2018 to 2025
42% Montréal growth among selected major cities
7% E-commerce share of total retail sales in 2025
$320B Approximate Ontario retail market size

Provincial Growth

Between 2018 and 2025, Quebec recorded approximately 39% retail sales growth while maintaining a substantial market size of approximately $187 billion.

Some smaller provinces and territories showed higher percentage growth, but their significantly smaller sales bases make them less comparable for a large-scale retail expansion.

Ontario remained Canada’s largest retail market at approximately $320 billion, with solid growth of around 32%.

Seasonal Sales Pattern

The analysis revealed a consistent seasonal pattern. Retail sales generally decline in February, rise steadily toward a peak in May, ease during the following months, and increase again near the holiday season.

This pattern can support inventory planning, staffing decisions, and promotional scheduling.

City-Level Performance

Among the cities with seasonally adjusted data, Montréal recorded the highest growth at approximately 42%, followed by Toronto at 39% and Vancouver at 35%.

Montréal’s strong performance supports the broader provincial findings for Quebec.

E-commerce Growth

E-commerce’s share of total retail sales increased from approximately 2.6% in 2017 to 7% in 2025.

Although online sales accelerated sharply in 2020, the share remained elevated afterward, suggesting a long-term structural change rather than a temporary increase.

Business Recommendation

Based on the combination of market size, growth rate, and city-level performance, Quebec—particularly the Montréal area—emerged as the strongest candidate for further expansion analysis.

Ontario remains an attractive lower-risk option when overall market size is the primary consideration.

Regardless of the physical expansion location, the continuing growth of e-commerce suggests that the company should also strengthen its online sales strategy.

Project Limitations

This analysis is based primarily on retail sales volume and historical growth. It does not include several factors that would be necessary for a final expansion decision:

  • Competitive intensity
  • Population and demographic growth
  • Commercial real estate costs
  • Local purchasing power
  • Store operating expenses
  • Industry-specific demand

The findings should therefore be treated as an initial market-screening analysis rather than a final location recommendation.
